Quick start

Blue Heeler becomes useful over time. It watches for nearby Bluetooth and Wi-Fi broadcasts, then raises signals that repeatedly appear or match a supported tracker pattern.

  1. Allow Nearby devices. Bluetooth scanning cannot start without it. Allow notifications so alerts reach you while the app is in the background. Approximate location is optional.
  2. Leave the scanner running. One sighting is usually ordinary. Repeated sightings across rolling windows are more useful.
  3. Watch Alerts, not Seen. Seen counts normal nearby electronics too. Alerts is the smaller set that deserves review.
  4. Tap a card before acting. Review why it was flagged, the first/last sighting, signal trend, and device family.
Blue Heeler does not require a cloud account. Tracker observations and classifications are processed on your phone.

Free and Pro

Scanning, alerts, Car Hunt, Fox Hunt, Tripwire, trigger strings, sensitivity and Turn and Burn are free. From version 1.0.167, saving a log is free too. Pro is a one-time purchase through Google Play, with no subscription. It adds alert history across days, log Share, AI Triage, Pack multi-phone scanning, camera-alert capture and Granular Buckets.

Buy Pro from Settings, where Google Play shows the price for your country. On a new phone signed in to the same Google account, tap Restore to get it back without paying again.

Reading the home screen

Seen

Every distinct nearby signal observed in the current session. Most are harmless phones, earbuds, TVs, cars, and access points.

Alerts

Signals whose behaviour or known identity crossed the current alert threshold. Tap an alert card to see why.

Sens

How weak a signal can be and still make the list. Tap it to cycle Relaxed, Normal and Paranoid. Paranoid shows the most, including distant devices. Known trackers, flagged devices and trigger-string matches stay listed at every setting.

Scanning

To confirm Blue Heeler is really scanning, check for its ongoing notification. If the notification says your scan session limit was reached, tap Keep scanning.

Radar

A rough signal-strength view, not a measured map. Nearer the centre generally means a stronger signal, not a guaranteed physical distance.

Sort controls

Threat prioritises risk signals. Recent, Signal, and Name offer different ways to review a busy environment.

Top-row buttons

Car Hunt sweeps for trackers and powered devices close by, such as in a car. Tripwire learns what is around you, then alarms when something new arrives. Help opens the in-app guide. More holds Save log (free from version 1.0.167), Share log and AI Triage (Pro), Sensitivity, Trigger strings, History and Reset scan. Settings controls scanning and optional features. The Pack and Stop & Burn buttons appear only when those features are switched on.

Alerts and device cards

A device card combines identity, persistence, signal, and classification evidence. Treat the card as a lead, not a verdict.

SEPARATED

An AirTag or other Apple Find My device is broadcasting that it is away from its owner. That is the classic sign of a planted tracker, though it can also be someone’s lost item. Review it promptly and check Android’s unknown tracker alerts too. Other known trackers, such as Tile, SmartTag and Chipolo, show a TRACKER badge, which cannot tell whether they are away from their owner.

ROT + number

Blue Heeler associated multiple changing addresses with one likely device group. Rotation is common and is not proof of malicious intent.

5 min / first / last

The persistence window plus the first and latest observations help distinguish a passing device from a recurring one.

BLE / Wi-Fi and signal bar

The radio source and received strength. A longer bar usually means stronger reception, which often, but not always, means closer.

Trust, flag, and beep

Trust your own equipment so it stops raising alerts. Flag a device you are worried about: it stays near the top of the list and raises an alert when it is seen. Pin keeps a device in the list without extra alerts. Try beep appears only on known trackers and is best-effort. Most trackers, including AirTag, Tile and SmartTag, only beep for their registered owner, so expect it to fail on them. Blue Heeler cannot override owner protections.

Investigation modes

Car Hunt

Car Hunt lists every Bluetooth device it hears, with the strongest and closest (IN BAND) on top, and at the same time uses the phone’s magnetometer to sense powered electronics. Sweep the phone slowly along a car’s bodywork or a bag, pause between moves, and look for a steady stronger or weaker trend rather than one reading. Walls, metal, cars and people all bend the signal. Keep Car Hunt vibration off, because the motor disturbs the magnetic reading. The magnetic reading helps find powered devices but is not a tracker detector by itself. To follow one particular device, use Fox Hunt.

Fox Hunt

Open a device card and tap Find this device. The full-height meter shows that device’s live signal strength and flashes each time a packet arrives. Turn slowly: the direction where the signal peaks is the likely direction of the device. Antenna calibration uses a Bluetooth device you can see to learn how your phone’s reception changes as you turn, and corrects the meter for the rest of that hunt. It does not turn signal strength into an exact distance.

Tripwire

Tripwire records a baseline (five minutes by default), then alerts when something new enters the selected signal range. Choose Ignore, Low, High or Alarm for how loud a trip is, and adjust squelch for the environment. Rebuild the baseline after moving rooms or changing locations.

Turn and Burn

Turn and Burn is free and off by default. When you switch it on in Settings, a flame button in the top bar takes the place of Stop scanner. Tapping it and confirming Stop & Burn stops scanning, overwrites and deletes all of Blue Heeler’s data on the phone (settings, trusted and flagged devices, Pack key, history and logs), then closes the app. It is best-effort: phone storage can keep old copies of data, so for the strongest result follow it with a factory reset. It does not remove logs you saved to Downloads or shared with other apps, it does not uninstall the app, and it does not clear Android’s own records.

Pack: scan together (Pro)

Pack lets two or more of your phones scan together. Each phone broadcasts a small Bluetooth message with what it is seeing, so the Pack can compare readings and narrow down where a tracker is. Pack is a Pro feature and is off by default. Every phone in the Pack needs Pro and Pack Scan switched on.

  1. Open Settings and enable Pack Scan on each participating phone.
  2. Set the same Pack key on every phone. Type it, or tap Show QR on one phone and Scan QR on the others. Without a key the Pack runs unsigned and trust cannot be shared.
  3. Check that Pack members appear on the radar before relying on shared observations.
  4. Enable “Share trust across the pack” only if you want a trusted/flagged choice copied between authenticated Pack devices.
Pack messages are signed with your Pack key but not encrypted. Anyone nearby with a Bluetooth scanner can read them, including partial addresses of the devices your phone is alerting on and, if Blue Heeler has Android location permission, your phone’s approximate location. Turn Pack off when that matters. Pack also cannot give an exact position: keep the phones apart, run Antenna calibration in Settings, and treat the combined readings as guidance.

Drone Remote ID

Many newer drones broadcast Remote ID (also called Open Drone ID), a public signal that identifies the aircraft, and some countries require it. When Blue Heeler hears one of these broadcasts over Bluetooth, it raises a “Drone overhead (Remote ID)” alert and the card shows a DRONE badge, with the drone type and serial when the broadcast includes them.

Important limit: Blue Heeler cannot see analogue FPV video or control links, Remote ID sent only over Wi-Fi, or drones that broadcast nothing. An alert cannot tell you who is flying, why, or exactly where the drone is.

This is an awareness feature only. Blue Heeler does not control, jam or interfere with any aircraft. If a drone worries you, confirm what you are seeing and follow local guidance.

Permissions and privacy

Nearby Bluetooth and Wi-Fi

Required for radio scanning. Android may limit results when system scanning is disabled.

Notifications and vibration

Used for foreground scanning status and alerts while you are not looking at the app.

Approximate location

Optional. Android asks for it with the other permissions when you first start, and scanning works without it. It is used for GPS co-traveller detection, spotting devices you keep seeing in one place, noticing when you have stopped moving, and your position in a Pack. Precise location is never requested.

Camera

Optional and on demand. Used to scan a Pack QR code; Blue Heeler does not record video.

Notification access

Optional, Pro. Camera-alert capture reads notifications only from the security-camera apps you tick, and keeps them on your phone.

Internet

Present only because Google Play Billing needs it to load the Pro price and handle your purchase. Blue Heeler has no server and its own code makes no network calls. Your radio observations are not uploaded.

For the complete data-handling explanation, read the privacy policy.

Troubleshooting

Scanning will not start, or has stopped

  • Confirm Bluetooth and Wi-Fi scanning are enabled in Android.
  • Open Android App info → Permissions and allow Nearby devices.
  • Disable battery restrictions for Blue Heeler if background scanning stops.
  • By default Blue Heeler pauses after 8 hours of scanning and waits for you to tap Keep scanning in its notification. You can change this in Settings under Scan session length.
  • Return to Blue Heeler and restart the scan.

No devices, or far fewer than expected

  • Wait through a complete scan window; many devices advertise intermittently.
  • Move away from dense interference and retest.
  • Check that Android has not revoked Nearby-device permission.
  • Remember that some trackers stop broadcasting or use signals Blue Heeler cannot classify.

Too many alerts

  • Tap Sens to step down to Normal or Relaxed.
  • Trust your own headphones, watches, vehicles, and home equipment.
  • After leaving a crowded place, open More and tap Reset scan.
  • Use persistence and movement evidence instead of one strong signal.

Pack member missing

  • Keep both phones unlocked during initial setup.
  • Confirm every phone has Pro, Pack Scan is on, and the Pack key is identical.
  • Make sure Bluetooth permission is granted on both phones.
  • Move the phones within normal Bluetooth range, then switch Pack Scan off and on again.

Safety and limits

Radio conditions are noisy. Walls, bodies, metal, phone antennas, traffic, and other transmitters can change readings. Some legitimate devices persist or rotate addresses.

A quiet screen is not an all clear. Some trackers broadcast rarely or look like ordinary devices, trackers that use only GPS and mobile data send nothing Blue Heeler can hear, and a paused or stopped scan sees nothing at all. No alerts does not mean no tracker.

If you feel in immediate danger: move to a safe public place, contact local emergency services or a trusted person. Do not confront the person you suspect.

Use Blue Heeler alongside Android’s built-in unknown-tracker alerts and the safety instructions supplied by Apple, Google, Tile, Samsung, or the relevant tracker maker. Keep screenshots or saved logs only when it is safe to do so (from version 1.0.167 saving a log is free; sharing needs Pro; screenshots always work). If you find a tracker, think before you disable or destroy it: it may be evidence, and the person may notice when it stops reporting. Police or a family violence service can help you decide what to do.

Frequently asked questions

Does an alert prove someone is tracking me?

No. It means Blue Heeler found a signal pattern worth reviewing. Confirm it using movement, persistence and platform safety alerts, and search physically only when it is safe.

If there are no alerts, am I safe?

Not necessarily. A quiet screen is not an all clear. Blue Heeler only hears trackers that broadcast over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi while it is scanning, and some trackers are built to avoid notice. If you are worried, keep scanning over time, use Android’s unknown tracker alerts too, and talk to police or a support service.

Why does Seen contain so many devices?

Modern environments are full of Bluetooth and Wi-Fi broadcasts. Seen is an observation count; Alerts applies additional rules to highlight a smaller set.

Can Blue Heeler locate a tracker exactly?

No. Car Hunt and Fox Hunt show relative signal changes. They cannot provide a guaranteed direction, distance, or location.

Can Blue Heeler make every tracker beep?

No. The command is best-effort. Tracker firmware, ownership protections, connection state, and supported protocols determine whether it works.

Does Blue Heeler upload my scans?

No. Blue Heeler has no server and does not upload your radio observations. Data leaves your phone only when you choose: Share log and AI Triage hand the log to an app you pick, and Pack broadcasts Pack messages over Bluetooth to phones nearby. Google Play handles the Pro purchase under Google’s own privacy policy.

How do I export a session?

Open More and choose Save log to Downloads (free from version 1.0.167) or Share log (Pro). The saved log includes your saved alert history with dates, and History has its own Save and Share buttons. AI Triage shares the same log, formatted for an AI assistant you pick; Blue Heeler does not send it anywhere itself. Whatever app you share with receives the log, so check it before you send. A log saved to Downloads stays there until you delete it, even after Turn and Burn.
